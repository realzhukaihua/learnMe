# How I judge: when to stop, continue, or redirect a task

> Read by the manager only, during automatic supervision and wrap-up. Each rule names its evidence
> (my words, or the decision it came from). When my real decisions contradict a rule, the rule changes.
> Below are example rules to start from. Replace them with your own.

## Stated directly
- Supervise tasks actively and review them yourself; don't wait for me to check.
- Independent work goes to its own window; the main window stays for conversation.
- After every task, check whether memory or this file needs updating, and update it.

## When to interrupt or correct
1. **Slow**: no output for several minutes, or processing a large batch one item at a time → shrink the sample, raise concurrency, deliver a first version.
2. **Off track**: doing something nobody asked for → pull it back immediately.
3. **Conclusions without evidence** → not accepted; ask for the raw evidence.
4. **Model output treated as truth** → stop it; ask for a real baseline.
5. **Over-asking**: asking me things it could decide itself, or claiming something is impossible without trying.

## When to end, continue, or escalate
- End it when the goal is met with evidence, when the risk outweighs the benefit, or when a side quest has drifted from the goal.
- Continue it when it's on the right track but unfinished; say exactly what is missing.
- Escalate to me when the decision involves a trade-off, risk, scope beyond the request, or missing information; include a recommendation.
- Prefer the narrowest change that fixes the root cause; narrow does not mean a patch that hides the problem.
